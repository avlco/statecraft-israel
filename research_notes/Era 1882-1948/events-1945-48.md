# Events of 1945-1948: incomplete research coverage

Research draft, 2026-10-25. Refs #93. Sources were read by AI agents on that date;
a person's factual check is pending. This is a facts-only research note, not event
YAML, card text, a choice of gameplay values or a sensitivity decision.

## Scope and evidence convention

The [research ruling on #93](https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel/issues/93#issuecomment-6340381721)
opens dates, short factual summaries and sources. Code, game content and decision
scenes remain paused. The [owner's scope and read-label confirmation](https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel/issues/93#issuecomment-6397045275)
applies to this draft. All 15 named anchors and five contexts below now have
limited source footholds; none is a complete event history or a completed issue.
The approximate wider coverage remains unresolved.

The coverage list comes from the issue and
[the Yishuv design, sections 6 and 10.2](https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel/blob/a1e2237663646482f44701f799999c42bc4be3a8/docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-10-era-yishuv-design.md).
Those are scope instructions, not historical evidence. The issue's "about 30"
and the design's approximate 15+15 are estimates, not a supplied list of 30
separate events. The three named decision scenes are already among the anchors
below. Neither splitting an anchor into cards nor combining overlapping anchors
is decided here.

**Agent-read** below means that the stated source extent was opened and read by
an AI agent, not that a person has fact-checked it. **GAP** means that the fact or
source has not been established in this note. A document date, planned release,
actual release and later editorial summary are kept separate. Conflicting values
will stay visible with their sources rather than being silently reconciled.

## 2. N05: Anglo-American Committee, a partial source foothold

The following are different stages, not interchangeable dates:

| Date | Narrow finding and attribution | Source and locator; agent-read 2026-10-25 |
|---|---|---|
| 20 April 1946 | The proposed presidential statement embedded in Truman's message to Attlee of 3 October 1946 dates the unanimous Committee report to this day. | [AAC-2](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1946v07/d548), paragraph beginning "The unanimous report". |
| 25 April 1946 | The Acting Secretary of State's circular telegram says publication is planned for 1 May. **GAP:** the complete original Committee report was not opened. These US records
support the limited attribution above, not a complete account of its inquiry,
recommendations, reception or implementation.

## 5. N10: Resolution 181, a limited yearbook reading

The UN yearbook records adoption at the 128th plenary meeting on 29 November
1947, and a vote of 33 to 13 with 10 abstentions for the report including the Plan
of Partition with Economic Union. Its reproduced opening recommends adoption
and implementation of that plan. [UN-2](https://cdn.un.org/unyearbook/yun/pdf/1947-48/1947-48_268.pdf),
printed page 247, left-column paragraph beginning "The report", and right-column
section j introduction and paragraph beginning "Recommends"; agent-read 2026-10-25.

**GAP:** the full resolution and plan were not read. Territorial details, legal
effect, implementation and Agency response remain unestablished; section 8
does not close those gaps.

## 6. N04: Black Sabbath, retrospective date evidence

The Palmach heritage article dates the British opening of Operation Agatha,
known as Black Sabbath, to 29 June 1946. [BS-1](https://www.palmach.org.il/history/database/?itemId=5239),
paragraph beginning with that date. The official IDF Archive biography of Assaf
Shimhoni also dates his arrest with others to that day and calls it
Black Sabbath. [BS-2](https://archives.mod.gov.il/FocusOn/Pages/041115.aspx),
biographical paragraph containing 29 June 1946. Both agent-read 2026-10-25.

**GAP:** these are institutional retrospectives, not an opened contemporary order
or arrest register. No totals, exact time, release dates or detention duration
are established here. The attempted Hansard access timed out; no parliamentary
announcement date is taken from a search snippet.

## 8. N02: a conditional Agency position, not a later vote

An official FRUS editorial note reports that on 2 October 1947 Silver, speaking
for the Jewish Agency, expressed willingness to accept the UNSCOP majority
report, subject to further constitutional and territorial discussion.
[PART-1](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1947v05/d809), final
two paragraphs, especially the paragraph beginning "Rabbi Silver"; agent-read
2026-10-25. The year follows the 1947 volume context.

**GAP:** the original UN committee record cited by the editors was not opened.
This conditional position on the UNSCOP report is not proof of unconditional
acceptance of Resolution 181 on 29 November, and establishes no implementation.

## 9. N03 and N15: the declaration's date and stated arrangement

The [Avalon transcription](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/20th_century/israel.asp)
places the declaration in Tel Aviv, 14 May 1948 (heading and attestation).
"ACCORDINGLY WE" identifies People's Council declarants. "WE DECLARE" assigns
provisional Council of State and Government roles to the Council and People's
Administration from the Mandate's termination.
DEC-1, these paragraphs; agent-read 2026-10-25.

**GAP:** original signed image and Israeli-archive corroboration were not read;
founding date, size, a 12 May vote and implementation remain unverified. The
issue/spec's 12 April 1948 opening is a game-design window, not a declaration-derived
historical date. The Knesset location notice was not declaration evidence.

## 10. Additional partial footholds and sensitive contexts

Numbered locators in this section refer to lines of the rendered web text,
not numbered paragraphs in the original document. Stable headings or phrases
identify the same passages; the source register records the extent actually read.

### N01: Resistance Movement — activity and authorization are distinct

[RM-1](https://palmach.org.il/history/database/?itemId=5204) (Palmach institutional retrospective, main text lines 65–69)
frames activity as October 1945–July 1946, dates transfer of approval authority
to an Agency committee to 6 December 1945, and an expanded committee to late
January 1946. These are different stages, not a verified founding day.
[RM-2](https://palmach.org.il/history/database/?itemId=5244) (same institution, overview line 66 versus body line 67)
prints first-half December 1945 in the overview but December 1946 in the body.
This internal year conflict remains unresolved. **GAP:** original minutes and
formation records were not opened; the two pages are not independent corroboration.

### N08: Exodus — departure is not return to Europe

[EX-1](https://www.kkl.org.il/education/today_in_history/exodus-18-7-1947.aspx) (KKL, body lines 62–64) dates departure from southern France
to 11 July 1947, the clash to the morning of 18 July, and transfer at Haifa to
deportation ships that night. [EX-2](https://www.palmach.org.il/history/database/?itemId=5072) (Palmach, opening departure text line
133) identifies Sète and 11 July. These institutional retrospectives do not make
the 18 July heading a return-to-Europe date.

[EX-3](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/exodus-1947) (USHMM, article lines 123–140, especially 127 and 137–139)
describes the 18 July struggle, Haifa transfer to three transports, a French stop
and subsequent transport to Hamburg and camps in Germany. **GAP:** exact later
stage dates, original ship records and disputed casualty descriptions are not
established here; no casualty total or causal claim about the UN is adopted.

### N11: registration, plans and recruitment

[REC-1](https://archives.mod.gov.il/Exhib/tzavgiyus/Pages/default.aspx) (IDF Archive exhibition narrative, lines 34, 38 and 46)
dates creation of the centre it calls מרכז המפקד לשרות העם to 19 November 1947.
It dates announcement A to 28 November, calling men and women aged 17–25 for
registration/classification set for 9 December, and separately gives an official
recruitment date of 1 February 1948.

[REC-2](https://archives.mod.gov.il/Exhib/giyusidf/Pages/default.aspx) (another IDF Archive exhibition, line 33) describes a
19 November meeting chaired by Golda Meyerson and a plan to announce on
1 December and open recruitment at Hanukkah a week later. It names the centre
מרכז המפקד לשירותי הציבור. Planned and issued announcements, registration and
service are not interchangeable; the differing names remain attributed.
**GAP:** original posters/protocols were not read, no Gregorian Hanukkah date is
computed, and no statutory conscription law or universal service-entry date follows.

### N12: selected convoy entries, not complete coverage

The Palmach institutional chronology [CONV-2](https://www.palmach.org.il/history/database/?itemId=5607) (main body lines
193–204) lists attacks on Jerusalem-bound convoys on 21, 22 and 24 March 1948,
an arrival on 25 March, an attacked return convoy near Nebi Daniel on 27 March,
and the Hulda convoy on 31 March. This is a selected sequence, not an exhaustive
inventory or a claim that all listed routes had the same destination.

[CONV-1](https://www.palmach.org.il/history/database/?itemId=5609) (Hulda article, main text lines 98–99) describes departure
from Hulda towards Jerusalem on the morning of 31 March 1948, mud and an attack,
followed by an order to retreat to Hulda. **GAP:** original orders and a full
convoy list remain unopened; casualty totals, disputed accounts and a ranked
explanation of failure are not adopted.

### N13: Plan Dalet — research attribution, not original order

Ze'ev Elron's IDF-hosted research article [PD-1](https://www.idf.il/%D7%90%D7%AA%D7%A8%D7%99-%D7%99%D7%97%D7%99%D7%93%D7%95%D7%AA/%D7%94%D7%9E%D7%97%D7%9C%D7%A7%D7%94-%D7%9C%D7%94%D7%99%D7%A1%D7%98%D7%95%D7%A8%D7%99%D7%94/%D7%99%D7%A1%D7%95%D7%93%D7%95%D7%AA/%D7%99%D7%A1%D7%95%D7%93%D7%95%D7%AA-1-1/%D7%92%D7%95%D7%A3-%D7%94%D7%AA%D7%9B%D7%A0%D7%95%D7%9F-%D7%91%D7%9E%D7%98%D7%9B-%D7%9C/) (section on the
planning department in Operations, line 53 and footnote 17) dates publication
of Plan Dalet to 10 March 1948 and describes its aim as control, on the Mandate's
termination, of territory allotted to the Jewish state by the UN decision.
**GAP:** the original order, clauses, local implementation and footnote 17
books were not read. This HTML research attribution, not a viewed PDF or
original order, settles neither disputed intent nor sensitivity classification.

### N14: Nachshon — keep the competing intervals attributed

[NACH-1](https://www.palmach.org.il/history/database/?itemId=5610) (Palmach article, teaser line 99 and body line
118) gives 3–5 April 1948 in its teaser but prints `15-3` April 1948 in its body.
The latter printed interval is retained, not silently repaired. Both passages
state an objective of opening the road to Jerusalem.

[NACH-2](https://www.idf.il/%D7%90%D7%AA%D7%A8%D7%99-%D7%99%D7%97%D7%99%D7%93%D7%95%D7%AA/%D7%A2%D7%A0%D7%A3-%D7%90%D7%99%D7%AA-%D7%9F/%D7%90%D7%99%D7%A8%D7%95%D7%A2%D7%99%D7%9D-%D7%91%D7%97%D7%A7%D7%99%D7%A8%D7%94/%D7%91%D7%93%D7%A8%D7%9A-%D7%9C%D7%99%D7%A8%D7%95%D7%A9%D7%9C%D7%99%D7%9D/) (IDF Eitan retrospective, lines 35 and 39) distinguishes
early-April preparations from the operation itself, beginning on the night of
5–6 April 1948 and ending on 15 April. The page's 7 April event header belongs
to its separate disappearance incident (line 42), not the operation's start.
**GAP:** no original order or authoritative reconciliation of these intervals was
read; no deployment totals, casualties or broad causal conclusion is adopted.

### C01–C05: facts-only sensitive contexts

- **C01, King David Hotel:** the IDF institutional retrospective [KD-1](https://www.idf.il/אתרי-יחידות/80-שנה-לפלמ-ח/תאריכון/יולי-ספטמבר/2271946-פיצוץ-משרדי-הממשלה-במלון-המלך-דוד-סוף-תנועת-המרי/)

  9 April and killings including women and children. This is an attributed

  line 79) also describes that date and the two organizations; the book

- **C03, Arab flight:** Wasson's assessment dated 3 May 1948, [FL-2](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1948v05p2/d206)

  (FRUS editorial note, Haifa lines 7–9) summarizes telegrams of 22 and
  26 April concerning Arabs leaving Haifa; those original telegrams were not
  opened. **GAP:** these limited readings do not establish countrywide coverage,
  totals, uniform causes or individual motives.
- **C04, Hadassah convoy:** Hadassah's retrospective commemoration [HD-1](https://www.hadassah.org/story/76-years-after-hadassah-convoy-massacre-honoring-the-victims)

  central Jerusalem towards its Mount Scopus hospital on 13 April 1948, stopped
  and attacked en route. **GAP:** no toll or full sequence is adopted; attempted
  access to a contemporary UN document failed and it was not read.
- **C05, Kfar Etzion:** the Ben-Gurion Archive's HTML diary transcription
  [KE-1](https://bengurionarchive.bgu.ac.il/node/9121) (13 May 1948, archival number 224024, line 86) records
  a report that Kfar Etzion had fallen; it is not a manuscript-image reading.
  The heritage chronology [KE-2](https://www.etzion-bloc.co.il/תמצית-סיפור-גוש-עציון) (entries for 12–14 May, lines
  164–166) places the Kfar Etzion battle on 13 May and remaining bloc defenders'
  captivity on 14 May. **GAP:** do not assign one date to every locality;
  exact times, tolls, responsibility and the full sequence remain unverified here.
